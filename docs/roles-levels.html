Reweights. Adds nothing. Same instrument at every level.

If seniority added parameters, an entry candidate would be measured on a different instrument from a director and the two scores would not be comparable. Instead every level scores the same things and weights them differently — so 62 at entry and 62 at director both mean "met the bar for the level".

Read the two ends of that table

At entry: proof of work 21%, skills 19%, education 17% — 57% of the score is what they've learned and built, because there is no track record yet.

At C-level: role pack 25%, scope 21%, industry 16% — and education has fallen to 3.1%, proof of work to 2.6%. Nobody hires a CEO on their GitHub. The instrument is identical; only the emphasis moved.

Fourteen roles

Two guardrails on pairing

Pair a leadership pack with a junior level, or an IC pack with C-level, and the system warns rather than blocks.

It tells you the pairing looks wrong and why — "most candidates will score near zero on these parameters" — then scores anyway. Odd pairings are sometimes correct (a founding engineer, a technical CEO). That is the recruiter's call to make, not the tool's to refuse.

The caveat, again, because it matters more as this grows

1,512 configurations is a lot of surface area, and none of these weights is empirically validated.

They encode considered judgement about relative importance — defensible in a room, not measured against outcomes. The bigger this gets, the more important it is to run graph.parameter_separation() once you have real hires behind it, and let the data delete the parameters that were never doing any work.
