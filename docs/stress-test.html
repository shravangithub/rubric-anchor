What this is actually doing

An AI ranking CVs is comparing them against each other. That means the order it gives you depends partly on the order you handed them over. Ask three times with the pile shuffled and most candidates land in roughly the same place — but some jump across your shortlist line.

Those are the ones where the AI never really had an answer. Their outcome came down to which single run you happened to use. This tool just finds them, so a person can look properly instead of leaving it to chance.

What to do with the result: the settled ones need nothing. For each unsettled candidate, check their biggest claim actually appears in their CV, score them on their own against your rubric, and have a named person decide. That's usually a handful of people, not the whole pile.

Honest limits: this catches candidates the AI is unsure about. It won't catch ones it's confidently wrong about. And three runs finds about three quarters of the genuinely borderline cases, not all of them — so never send an automatic rejection either way.
